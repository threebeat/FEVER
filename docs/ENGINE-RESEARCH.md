# FEVER — What other engines can teach us

The first release has an unusually good test room. In the kennel aisle, a bar can be a one-pixel line, a sound barrier and the outline of the thing about to reach Reed. A graphical shortcut that erases the bar or an AI shortcut that ignores its enclosure changes the encounter. The choice of technology follows from what the player must be able to see, hear and infer.

## What shipped games actually did

| Production | Documented decision | Consequence for FEVER |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bodycam** | Reissad describes dense scene dressing, image degradation and a relatively simple lens treatment alongside UE5's lighting. Those effects address different problems. | Use the lamp to reveal convincing edges, fasteners and surface finishes first. Keep lens distortion optional, noise low and UI sharp. Run the kennel sightline with both optical effects disabled. A dog should still read as a physical animal in an enclosure. [Developer interview](https://80.lv/articles/developing-a-game-with-realistic-graphics-in-unreal-engine) |
| **Metro 2033** | 4A's engine retrospective explains why dynamic lighting and deferred shading were central to a story told largely through changes in light. | Our current room has up to eight practicals and one shadow-casting flashlight. Forward rendering keeps those few lights and the PSX path simple. Capture GPU cost on the Christmas office before adding many overlapping lights. If the light loop dominates, try lists of lights per tile or room before rewriting the entire renderer. [4A engine retrospective](https://enginearchitecture.org/downloads/REAC_2024_4A.pdf) |
| **INSIDE** | Playdead published separate work on scene rendering, stutter-free streaming and temporal antialiasing. Its released temporal implementation requires motion-vector information for independently moving geometry. | Test *frame pacing* separately from average throughput. An optional photographic AA experiment needs correct gun, hand and tentacle motion vectors plus history rejection through a door or exposure jump. Compare readability of bars in movement. PSX retains its intentional snapping. [Playdead presentations](https://blog.playdead.com/articles/inside_presentations/inside_publications.html) · [Published implementation](https://github.com/playdeadgames/temporal) |
| **Sunset Overdrive** | Insomniac standardized trim layouts, normal information and UV tooling to texture a large environment with a small art team. | Share surfaces and material controls across the cage banks. Use unique texture space for evidence labels. The current cooker emits mipmapped, block-compressed arrays. The next environment kit should reserve a measured trim strip for doorframes, hinges and cart rims. Check both close highlights and distant shimmer. [Insomniac's production talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022323/The-Ultimate-Trim-Texturing-Techniques) · [Adobe trim-sheet tutorial](https://www.adobe.com/learn/substance-3d-painter/web/trim-sheets-with-substance-painter) |
| **Mirror's Edge, Halo 2 and Destiny** | DICE tested how full-body moves read from the player's camera. Halo's authoring guide names breathing, prolonged-idle variation, overlays, ready, firing and reload families. Bungie's David Helsby discusses how persistent first-person motion can feel physical while staying responsive. | Anchor Reed's pelvis and boots in world space. Let the chest mount turn relative to the hips. Layer small breathing, an occasional grip shift and weapon-specific action timing. Inspect a world-space planted boot, an interrupted reload and a sudden aim input from Reed's camera. [DICE](https://www.ea.com/amp/news/the-world-from-faiths-perspective) · [Halo animation guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/halo-master-chief-collection/h2/art/animation/animationsfpanims) · [Bungie](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022297/The-Art-of-First-Person) |
| **Left 4 Dead** | Valve's bots use a representation of walkable space. Flow distance helps answer “ahead” and “behind” along a twisting route. A separate director estimates intensity and changes encounter frequency. | The first call can use a collision-aware floor grid and authored landmarks. It already tests a squadmate waiting, catching up, resuming defense and getting past an obstacle. A stacked hospital needs distance along its navigation route before two actors on different floors can meaningfully be called “close.” Keep pacing decisions outside individual actors. [Valve's AI systems slides, navigation and pacing sections](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf) |
| **Amnesia: The Bunker and Gloomwood** | Frictional's creative and design leads describe a creature responding to noise and using the structure of the bunker. Gloomwood developer Dillon Rogers discusses sound propagation and its gameplay role. | Let the host and the player use the same door and barrier data. A host can chase a visible actor or investigate a recently heard location. Closing a door changes how strong a sound reaches each listener. The design test is whether a player can repeat an action and infer what drew the creature. [Frictional developer discussion](https://www.aiandgames.com/p/how-the-beast-works-in-amnesia-the) · [Rogers interview, sound chapter at 3:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BRsWvtJSm0U&t=228s) |
| **Overgrowth** | Wolfire's custom Phoenix engine supported procedural animation and an integrated AngelScript layer, with modding an explicit motivation. | Keep the contact solver, action clocks and pose blending native. The current night's cue sheet, material table and tuning file already allow iteration without compiling. If mission authors need to create new verbs repeatedly, test one sandboxed event VM against a second authored call and save/replay compatibility. This is the point at which its additional complexity could pay for itself. [Wolfire engine notes](https://wiki.wolfire.com/wiki/Phoenix_Engine) · [Source release](https://www.wolfire.com/blog/2022/04/Overgrowth-Open-Source-Announcement/) |

## Tools that solve a specific production problem

| Problem | Tool and place in the pipeline | Trial and acceptance condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is a streak coming from a wall, its shadow or the recorder? | **RenderDoc**, a desktop developer tool for D3D11. Capture our executable and inspect the depth map, HDR scene and lens output in order. | At the kennel capture, inspect the same pixel at each step. A post-process change should fix an optical issue without moving the physical shadow. [Frame capture guide](https://github.com/baldurk/renderdoc/blob/v1.x/docs/getting_started/quick_start.rst) |
| Is a detailed model consuming bandwidth for invisible surfaces? | **FEVER asset audit** is in the repository and CI. **meshoptimizer** is a candidate offline step for new sculpted imports. It supports attribute-aware simplification and locked vertices. | Run the audit after cooking. For a simplifier pilot, preserve material seams, contact points and motion correspondence. Compare animation extremes and disconnected components as well as silhouettes. [Current audit](../tools/audit-assets.py) · [meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer) |
| How do we get production materials into GPU memory? | The current cooker uses Pillow offline and writes native BC1/BC3 DDS arrays with all mips. **DirectXTex / texconv** is a Windows authoring alternative as the texture set grows. | Pilot BC7 on a photographed enamel/label prop and BC5 on a dedicated normal map. Compare compressed bytes, lamp highlights and noise at oblique angles. The existing array loader accepts its two shipped formats. A new format needs a loader change and a reference frame. [DirectXTex](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectXTex) · [Material parameter reference](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html) |
| How do artists make a reusable asset? | Blender or a sculpting package for upper-body cloth breaks, contact edges and silhouette. A high-to-low bake transfers shallow detail onto the runtime mesh. The current editable procedural source and cooker remain available. | Put the asset in the *in-game* lens at the closest distance and in the shadow pass before adding topology. Test matte, wet and painted areas under a moving lamp. [Professional prop workflow](https://thegnomonworkshop.com/workshops/creating-props-for-games-vol-1) · [Thomas Butters' first-person art workflow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I-P7lxdyNeA) |
| What changes when maps get stairs and loops? | **Recast** bakes navigation offline. **Detour** queries the walkable surface at runtime. Keep FEVER's intent selection and actor-specific behaviors above that adapter. | Pilot two agents going to different floors, a blocked stair and a reopened door. Require reachable destinations, stable recovery and bounded path work before moving the campaign across. [Recast and Detour](https://recastnav.com/) |
| How do several rigs share a growing animation library? | **ozz-animation** provides native clip sampling and layered skeletal blending. It is a candidate for motion-captured human performances. | Port Cole’s examination of Eddie using the existing gameplay cue IDs. Compare planted hand drift, compressed clip bytes and CPU time through the transition from kneeling to recoil. Keep world-space foot contacts and the gear event clock in FEVER. [ozz-animation](https://github.com/guillaumeblanc/ozz-animation) · [Blend sample](https://github.com/guillaumeblanc/ozz-animation/blob/master/samples/blend/README.md) |

## What to measure on the next hardware pass

1. Record the adapter, driver, window size, visual mode and frame times after warm-up. Walk the same approach, aim through the cage wires, fire during the lash, then look down at Reed's boots. Use `FEVER.exe --benchmark` for a fixed 1080p comparison of GPU and CPU work. Use F3 for ordinary player-paced traversal. Capture the 95th and 99th percentile as well as the mean. Run a cold entry separately to catch loading hitches.
2. Measure the flashlight shadow, opaque scene and lens pass independently in a GPU profiling capture. A CPU-bound run calls for an actor, submission or allocation trace. A fill-bound run calls for a light-overlap or pixel-cost experiment. Changing display resolution before identifying the bottleneck can hide the wrong problem.
3. Compare diagnostic frames with grain off, then turn it back on. Inspect actual silhouette error, sparkle on cage bars, glove contact, boot penetration, labels and material identity. Check the open/closed-door audio reference at matched playback level.
4. Once the second full chapter exists, time how long it takes to add one new behavior, a material and a voiced line. That evidence should decide whether to pay for an editor, a scripting VM or a general animation library. The first chapter already has a reproducible scene, cooked assets, a repeatable playthrough and a Windows graphics check for comparison.
